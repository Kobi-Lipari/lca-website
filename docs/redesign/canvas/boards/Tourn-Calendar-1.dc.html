Notes on this board

C1Weekend by weekend

  1. 1Weekend ribbon. Chess runs on weekends, so the calendar is 13 weekends, not 92 days. Today is named above it. Tap one and the list jumps there; the navy cell follows your scroll.
  2. 2Empty weekends. Quiet weekends shrink to one line, so a parent sees at a glance which Saturdays are free, and organizers see a gap to fill.
  3. 3Bell. The bell shows on hover or focus, at the same spot on every row; it never pushes the row’s content.
  4. 4Preview. B’s preview takes the rail only while a row is selected; closing it brings back A’s rail.
  5. 5Deadline strip. Deadlines sit inside the weekend they lead up to, as one line of text, not as marks on a grid.

C2Month beside the list

  1. 1Rail calendar. Tap a day or a bar and the list scrolls to that week. Bars have no words; the names are in the list beside it.
  2. 2Season strip. Counts per month in one line. A month outside the When range sets When to that month.
  3. 3Legend. Calmer than Tourn-C: two thin lanes at most, no deadline ticks, no club nights, no layers. The shaded row follows your scroll.
  4. 4Signed-out bell. Signed out, the bell asks you to log in or create an account; nothing is collected first.
  5. 5Weeks start Monday, so a Sat–Sun event stays in one week section and one bar.

C3Three months at a glance

  1. 1Overview. The three months are the When chip. Change When and the blocks change; Next moves the whole window.
  2. 2Weekend pill. One count per weekend, in words. Navy means an LCA event that week; the word LCA says so too.
  3. 3Signed-in bell. All four reminder options, one week and one day before ticked. An option that can’t apply is greyed with the reason, as on the decided List board.
  4. 4Preview. B’s preview only after a row is picked; A’s rail before that. Drawn here for a partner event: no count, no Register, the organizer’s site instead.
  5. 5One list. The list never splits by the overview: it runs on across month breaks, which the evidence favours over a grid.

The same in all three

  • Decided header with the rook mark; signed out it reads Donate · Log in · Join LCA, signed in Donate · My LCA.
  • Toolbar: Upcoming 25 | Results; List · Calendar · Map · Table centred with Calendar on; search to its right; When · Type · Region chips; 16 tournaments, removable chips, Clear all, Copy link to this view.
  • LCA rows: LCA event, status in words, X of Y registered, Register. Partner rows: Partner event and “Registers on the organizer’s site ↗”, never Register, never a count.
  • The bell sits in the last cell of every row, shown on hover or focus (always on phones).
  • Deadlines are one line of text with a clock, never marks on a grid.

C1

Weekend by weekend

The calendar is the list itself: one block per weekend from Sat, Oct 10 to Sun, Jan 3, each holding that weekend’s events, with a 13-weekend ribbon on top that jumps the list.

Frame 1440 · signed out · Baton Rouge Classic selected

Best for

Players and parents asking “what’s on this Saturday, and which Saturdays are free?” It matches how chess actually happens (weekends) and keeps the dated list as the main thing, which the Hund, Dowell and Mueller evidence favours.

Trade-off

Not a calendar most people would recognise (no month grid), so “Calendar” may feel close to List. Midweek events (Thu, Oct 22) live inside the following weekend’s block. The longest page of the three, because every weekend gets its own block.

Feedback applied

Calmer than Tourn-C because there is no grid at all: the only calendar-shaped element is one row of 13 labelled cells, with no bars, ticks, layers or frames. B’s preview takes the rail only after a row is picked (A’s rail otherwise, stated under the pane).

Other widths and phones

At 1440 the ribbon fits 13 cells; a longer When range scrolls it sideways with arrow buttons at each end. Below 1200px the rail drops under the list and the preview opens as a sheet. Phones (later board): the ribbon becomes a sticky scroller of 72px weekend chips under the app bar, the date column becomes a 44px sticky weekend header, rows use Tourn-Phones cards, and the preview opens full screen.
