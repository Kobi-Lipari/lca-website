## Why this order

- **The rules first.** Every later step asks `domain/events/creation.ts` who may create, charge, name or delete; writing the rules once, with a unit test that walks the brief's table grant by grant with the constant set and unset, means no handler decides anything itself. The record opens in the same step so each later step fills in a line that already exists.
- **The migration before the create.** The integration harness applies every migration to each test file's database, so the columns must exist before any test of steps 3 to 6 runs; and the tournament column reaches every strict contract through `toTournamentResponse`, which spreads the row, so the contracts and the trim's manager-only list change in the same step as the columns.
- **The create before the save.** A standing director's created event, with its director row, is what the fee rule, the naming, the delete and the notice are tested on; step 3 makes it real through the handler rather than seeding it.
- **The fee rule, then naming and deleting, then the notice.** The three server rules on an existing event are independent; the fee rule comes first because its words are G1's and its comparison needs the most care, and the notice comes last of the three because its helper is what the console part's scheduled phase will call.
- **The endpoints before the pages.** The pages read `GET /api/me/tournament-creation` and post `/api/me/payout-request`; building them first means the pages are built and tested against real answers, and the contracts move the ratchet in one step.
- **The lists, then the manage page, then the notices.** Step 8 draws the buttons and the label and adds both wrappers with their fixtures; step 9 is the manage page's part alone, so neither step carries the other's files; and G1 and G2 come last because they read the grant answer and the payout request and close the part.
