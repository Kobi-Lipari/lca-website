Louisiana Rooted

statewide, with an accent

The idea

Rooted in place without the Mardi Gras costume: warm magnolia paper, a soft early-1900s serif like an old shop sign, and cypress, Gulf blue and brick as small accents. Parishes organize the site, with a parish map as navigation, and an ironwork LCA monogram appears once per page.

Best for

A rebrand that feels unmistakably Louisiana, and the one direction that makes the old logo's purple and green look intentional.

Watch out for

Kitsch creeps in fast: no fleur-de-lis, beads or alligators. Fraunces has no tabular figures, so it stays in headlines, and parish data must exist before the map can navigate.

09

Do and don't

Do
Let specifics carry the place: parish names, real venues, streetcar green. Mardi Gras colors appear only inside photos.
Do
Use the monogram once per page and keep accents small: a brick link, a Gulf-blue map, one cypress button.
Don't
Use fleur-de-lis, beads, alligators or purple-green-gold parade palettes anywhere in the interface.
Don't
Stack numbers in Fraunces (no tabular figures) or show rating losses in red: losses are muted.
