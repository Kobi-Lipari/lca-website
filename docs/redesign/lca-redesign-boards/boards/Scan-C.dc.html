How to write chess moves

Every move gets a short name. Learn the code in five steps.

1Every square has a name
87654321
e4
abcdefgh

Letter first, for the column (a to h). Then the number, for the row (1 to 8). The marked square is e4. White starts on rows 1 and 2.

2Pieces use capital letters
KKing
QQueen
RRook
BBishop
NKnight
nonePawn

The Knight is N, because K is taken by the King. A pawn move is just the square: e4.

3Piece + square = the move
Nf3Knight to f3
e4pawn to e4
Qh5Queen to h5
4Special marks
xtakes Bxc6 · exd5
O-Ocastles on the King's side
+check Qh5+
O-O-Ocastles on the Queen's side
#checkmate Rd8#
=Qpawn becomes a Queen e8=Q
5Read a famous finish

Paul Morphy of New Orleans, at the Paris opera in 1858:

16. Qb8+ Nxb8   17. Rd8#

Queen to b8, check! The knight has to take it. Rook to d8: checkmate.

Directors: print sheets already filled in from the pairings.
1

Corner marks. The scanner finds all four, then straightens and crops the photo before it reads a single move.

2

One box per move. Letter ticks keep handwriting apart, so fewer misreads; bigger boxes give 50 moves on the front and 51–100 on the back.

3

Printed from the pairings. Event, round, board and names come typed, and the QR holds a sheet ID, so a scan files the game under its pairing.

4

Result boxes. The scanner reads them and offers the result to the director in results entry; nothing posts without the TD.

5

The loop. The kids' sheet ends at the scanner: learn the code, write a game, scan it, study it. Morphy's Opera Game finish is the reading drill.

6

Two cards per sheet. One for the TD table, one with photo tips. Log in to scan, no stored photos and 20 scans a day are the scanner's rules today.

Prints on US Letter in black ink; nothing relies on colour. The blue handwriting is only there to show how the boxes are used.
