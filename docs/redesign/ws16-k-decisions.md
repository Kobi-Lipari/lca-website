What the research had established when it was stopped (four angles and the reader of the texts had finished; two skeptics had not; the journal is <scratchpad>/ws16/postal-address-research.journal.jsonl): the Act and 16 CFR Part 316 have no exemption for non-profits; the address duty (15 U.S.C. 7704(a)(5)(A)(iii)) applies only to a message whose primary purpose is commercial; the FTC said in 2005 and 2008 that an association's messages to its members are "likely" transactional or relationship messages, and that a newsletter a person is entitled to counts whether it is all information or mixes information with commercial content, unless the commercial content clearly exceeds what the person might reasonably have expected; an offer of a new or renewed membership to a lapsed member is commercial; the FTC says it cannot enforce against a body not organized for its own or its members' profit; states and internet access providers can sue a non-profit, for a missing address only on a "pattern or practice", with capped damages; no case was found against a non-profit or a small association; Amazon SES, Gmail, Yahoo and Microsoft do not ask for a postal address.
Guardrails this gives the newsletter part, at no cost: the subject line names the newsletter and is not a sales line; member news comes first in every issue; the newsletter is not used to sell membership to people whose membership has lapsed (a renewal reminder to them is a separate email).
