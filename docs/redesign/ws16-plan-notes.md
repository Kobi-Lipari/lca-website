- **The outside facts that shape the design** (read on Tuesday, November 10, 2026): Amazon answers a request for real sending within 96 hours; until then the account is in the sandbox (verified recipients and the mailbox simulator only, 200 a day, one a second). SES's v2 SendEmail takes the two one-click headers on simple content. SES reports bounces and complaints through SNS, EventBridge, Firehose, CloudWatch or Pinpoint, never straight to a web address, and it also emails every report to the sending address unless that forwarding is turned off. Amazon reviews an account at a 5% bounce rate or a 0.1% complaint rate and may pause it at 10% or 0.5%; Gmail passes it no spam reports. A message to an address on Amazon's suppression list is accepted and silently not delivered. SNS tries an HTTPS delivery three more times, 20 seconds apart, then discards it, unless a delivery policy stretches the tries to at most an hour. Resend's terms ask bulk mail for a postal address and a company name and for recipients who explicitly opted in; Resend suppresses an address that reports spam for every email of the account, receipts included, and may close an account above a 0.08% complaint rate; its free tier sends 100 emails a day. Gmail and Yahoo ask bulk senders for one-click unsubscribe and every sender for SPF or DKIM and a low spam rate. Workers Free allows 50 outside calls, 50 database queries and 10 ms of processor time an invocation, and a request over the processor limit ends with error 1102; Workers Paid allows 10,000, 1,000 and 30 seconds; `waitUntil` lasts 30 seconds after the answer on both. Cloudflare's Bot Fight Mode may challenge calls from other servers and cannot be skipped by a WAF custom rule or a Page Rule; the one exception Cloudflare names is an IP Access rule that matches the request first. Cloudflare's own email service is a beta for transactional mail only. Read again on Wednesday, November 11, 2026: Amazon's pricing page now describes plans, and new SES accounts start on the Essentials plan from Tuesday, July 21, 2026 ($0.16 per 1,000 emails, no fixed monthly fee; à la carte is $0.10 per 1,000), so the plan's volume costs cents a month on either; Yahoo's sender rules ask that a From domain exist in DNS with an A or MX record (a null MX is a reason to refuse mail); Amazon says a return-path (MAIL FROM) name must not be a name you send From and must be used for nothing else; and Amazon emails bounce and complaint notices to the From address unless email feedback forwarding is turned off.
- **The law, as K settled it** (decision 23): no postal address in any email, and no setting, slot, reminder or question for one. What the research had found when K stopped it: the address duty applies only to a message whose primary purpose is commercial; the FTC has said an association's messages to its members are likely transactional or relationship messages, a newsletter a member is entitled to among them; an offer of renewed membership to a lapsed member is commercial; and a commercial message must also be marked as an advertisement unless the person gave prior affirmative consent. The plan keeps to that footing: the newsletter goes to current members (K's rule, decision 28), it never sells membership to lapsed members (decision 23's guardrails, shown on the newsletter tab), and it carries the opt-out the FTC asks for, honored at once and working for at least 30 days after sending, with a choice that stops all optional mail.

## The design in plain words

Each choice, its reason, and the alternative turned down.

- **One seam, with the service chosen by a server constant.** Every email goes through `deliver()` in `functions/utils/mail/send.ts`, which hands it to exactly one service, the one `MAIL_SENDER` names (`'resend'` until the cutover). *Why:* one deploy decides one service, so no message can be sent by both, and a later service (Cloudflare's for event mail, decision 7) is one more transport. *Turned down:* an automatic fallback to Resend when SES answers with an error, because an answer lost after Amazon took the message would then send it twice; and a switch in Admin, because the brief keeps server behaviour in constants nothing outside the code can change.
- **The move first, byte for byte.** Step 1 moves both of today's Resend calls behind the seam and a test compares every request with the literal text today's code writes; that test, and the two suites that hold today's path, are pinned to it whatever the shipped constants say. *Why:* the move touches every email the site sends; proving it changed nothing makes every later change reviewable on its own. *Turned down:* moving and adding headers or SES in one step.
- **Every address in one place** (decision 28). `domain/mail/addresses.ts` holds every From address, display name and reply-to the site sends with: today's three values from step 1, each LCA list's sender from step 4. *Why:* K decided every address form once, together, from the lead's address map (decision 37), except where replies go (choice 6, open; replies stay with LCA's Gmail inbox as today), so the forms are written in one file, the newsletter's by step 4 and the tournament list's by step 11, and the switch-on change writes any that differ; event mail's bytes stay today's, which step 1's bytes test holds. *Turned down:* a constant in each mail file, as today, and hard-coding the newsletter's sender before K decides it.
- **Our own request signer.** About eighty lines of AWS Signature Version 4 on the Worker's Web Crypto, held to AWS's published vectors (recomputed while planning). *Why:* the worker's closure test refuses any package in the files around `email.ts`, and the signer is small. *Turned down:* `aws4fetch`, which Cloudflare's docs name for Workers, because it would be the one package in that closure.
- **SES's v2 SendEmail with simple content.** A JSON body with the subject, the HTML, an optional text part, up to 15 headers, the configuration set and tags. *Why:* the one-click headers are allowed on simple content, so no MIME is built. *Turned down:* raw MIME (more code for nothing gained) and SMTP (not open to a Worker).
- **The newsletter goes out only through Amazon SES** (question 2). While Resend sends, the newsletter can be written and tested, and its Send says it waits; a list message that meets Resend waits, untried. *Why:* Resend's terms ask bulk mail for a postal address, which decision 23 leaves out, and for recipients who opted in; Resend would suppress a family's receipts after one spam report on the newsletter; and it may close an account above a 0.08% complaint rate, which a single report on a newsletter of about 155 passes, and that account carries every receipt. *Turned down:* decision 11's paid month of Resend for the newsletter, and a second Resend account for it, since both still meet the address and opt-in terms.
- **The cutover waits for Workers Paid** (question 3). *Why:* signing adds processor time, and on the free plan's 10 ms a director's announcement to many entrants could be cut part way and then sent twice by a second press, since it has no rows to mark. *Turned down:* sending through the campaign engine for announcements, a bigger change to a page directors already use.
- **The row decides its path.** A campaign with a list is sent only by the list path (its footer, its headers, its gate, its mark before the send), whatever `MAIL_LISTS_ON` says. *Why:* the site and the worker deploy apart, and a revert of the switch-on change while a newsletter is going out must not send the rest of it the old way. *Turned down:* a status of its own for list campaigns, which the tables' CHECKs refuse without a rebuild.
- **Never send one message twice.** Under the engine's rules a campaign row is marked just before its send and is never claimed again once marked; a row whose claim expired with the mark on is written off with plain words; an answer that does not prove Amazon refused the message (no answer, a 500, a timeout) is never tried again; a throttle, a paused account, a key fault or a fault of the account's setup ends the run and puts the untried rows back. The worker's reminders and the registration-open notices claim their row with a conditional mark before the send and send only when the claim took, so two runs over the same rows send each once. *Why:* SES has no idempotency key, and today's code already loses answers: `waitUntil` ends 30 seconds after the answer while the request path drains for 60. *Turned down:* today's retry of every 5xx and network error. The cost is honest: a network blip leaves one person without that newsletter, with the words in the history so an admin can send a copy.
- **Each deployment checks its own copy of the link key.** A list campaign stores a 16-character check of the key it was made with, and a deployment whose key differs sends nothing and says so. *Why:* the worker's copy is typed apart from the site's, and the sweep sends most of a newsletter; a typo would break most unsubscribe links while K's test, signed by the site, passed.
- **Batch sizes per hosting plan.** A constant, `WORKERS_PLAN`, `'free'` until K confirms Workers Paid: 10 a request and 20 a sweep run on free, 40 a batch with a 20-second deadline checked before each send and 300 a sweep run on paid. Under the rules the daily run sends its reminders before its rating lookups, at most 25 on free, and a fetch the runtime refuses for the limit is tried again the next day. *Why:* decision 8. *Turned down:* today's 40 and 150, which pass the free plan's 50 outside calls.
- **Our own tables, keyed by address.** `mail_contacts` (one row per address, with the person's own stop-all mark and a spam report's mark kept apart), `mail_list_choices` (`in` or `out` per contact and list, with its source), `mail_lists` (the newsletter and LCA tournament email now, clubs in part two; each marked main mailer or not) and `mail_do_not_send` (dead addresses). *Why:* opt-outs must survive a change of service (decision 4); an address with no member account can hold a choice; a family shares one address, so one choice per address is one email per family; part two's sign-ups write `in` on lists that take sign-ups, never on the newsletter unless K says so, with no new table; and decision 18's rule needs "main mailer" on the list and a report's mark apart from the person's own choice. *Turned down:* columns on `members`, SES's own contact lists, and one mark for both kinds of stop.
- **The do-not-send list holds dead addresses only.** A permanent bounce stops all mail to the address; Amazon's guess from its paid validation does not. A spam report writes an `out` choice on the list it was pressed on and, by the setting, the report's mark, which stops every list that is not one of LCA's main mailers (decision 18); a report that says the message is not spam changes nothing. *Why:* decision 4 keeps event and account mail flowing whatever a person's choices. *Turned down:* putting a person who reported spam on the do-not-send list, which would stop their receipts and confirmations.
- **Amazon's own suppression list holds bounces only.** *Why:* with spam reports on it, one report on a newsletter would make Amazon silently drop that family's receipts. *Turned down:* Amazon's default of both.
- **Links signed with a key of their own, with no expiry.** A token is the contact's id, the list (or "p" for preferences) and a 132-bit signature under `MAIL_LINK_SECRET`; a previous key can be kept for checking during a change of key. *Why:* the FTC wants an unsubscribe to work for at least 30 days, RFC 8058 wants a part that cannot be forged, and WS06's short-lived tokens should be able to change their key without breaking a link in an old email. *Turned down:* WS06's `tokens.ts` with its expiry, and a random token stored per email.
- **Opening a link changes nothing.** The header's address takes the one-click POST that mail providers send, and a plain GET of it leads to the page; the footer's link opens a page with one button. Both pages are always routed and draw today's empty main area until a list email has gone. *Why:* mail scanners open links on their own; a revert must not break a link already sent. *Turned down:* unsubscribing on a plain visit, and pages that exist only while the switch is on.
- **The gate lives in the seam, with one query a loop.** Once the rules are on, and for list mail always, `deliver()` refuses a dead address and a reserved name before any service is called, and for list mail a person who turned that list off, stopped all optional mail or, for a list that is not a main mailer, reported spam; loops read their whole list's addresses in one query; a gate that cannot be read lets event mail through and holds list mail back. *Why:* no send path can forget it, the worker stays inside the free plan's query budget, and a missing table must not stop receipts. List mail and test sends also skip the example domains, where migration 0002's sixteen sample members live, so a first newsletter cannot hard-bounce on them.
- **The newsletter is a campaign with a list.** It reuses the claim, the sweep, the history and the recipient rows, with four endpoint methods of its own (show, send, test, stop) behind `requireListMailer`, a guard that reads the session once. *Why:* one engine (build once), and new endpoints ship with contracts without moving the pending list or the K1d row. *Turned down:* widening today's `POST /api/admin/campaigns`, which would need its contract and move a row a WS01 record test pins as history.
- **Group email's member groups wait for a list of their own.** While the switch is on and until step 11, "Groups of members" is not drawn; Specific people stays account mail for a note about a person's own membership, entry or request. Step 11 brings member groups back on the LCA tournament email list, a main mailer. *Why:* a message to a group of members is optional mail, which must carry an unsubscribe and honor a person's stop-all choice, and Group email has never been used on production. *Turned down:* member groups as account mail with no unsubscribe (the first draft's default).
- **The receiver listens to SNS and checks each signature.** It verifies the message under a certificate fetched over HTTPS from SNS's host for our region, requires our topic and our account, confirms only our topic's subscription, ignores a notice more than three hours old, answers 500 for a certificate it cannot read so SNS tries again, and writes with statements that a second copy cannot change. *Why:* it proves the notification came from our own topic without a shared secret, and it is Amazon's documented route. *Turned down:* EventBridge's API destination with a secret header, and the older identity notifications (no tags, so a spam report could not be tied to its list).
- **Only what is needed is stored.** For a bounce, the address, its kind and the time; for a spam report, the list's choice and the report's mark. No diagnostic text, headers, feedback report or network address. *Why:* rule 3.
- **The media manager is a board seat's holder** (question 4, which K settled), reaching only WS16's own endpoints, with a Group email link in the account menu: the newsletter from switch-on and member groups from checkpoint N, and not Specific people (question 4's open half). *Why:* less work and safer than a new role; today's campaign endpoints take typed-in addresses and hand-picked members, which read members' records beyond what sending needs (decision 14).
- **No postal address** (decision 23). The footer is built in one place, `domain/mail/footer.ts`, so a line could be added later in one small change.
- **Thin pages on today's pages** (decision 12, and decision 24's simple first version: today's text box, links pasted by hand), each with a named later home (the table below), and no new browser spec.
- **Previews never send.** No mail key, no link key and no topic on the Preview environment; tests answer every provider's address in the harness.
- **One configuration set, `lca-mail`, with tags for kind and list; one domain identity, `louisianachess.org`, whose default set is `lca-mail`; a return path (custom MAIL FROM) of our own, `bounce.louisianachess.org`, and `bounce.news.louisianachess.org` for LCA's lists, never `news.` or `clubs.` itself (Amazon: the return-path name must not be a name you send From and must be used for nothing else).** *Why:* one event destination and one set for the key's policy to name; K decided on Wednesday, November 11, 2026 (decision 37) that LCA's lists are signed apart from account and event mail, so `news.louisianachess.org` is its own sending domain with its own Easy DKIM records now and `clubs.louisianachess.org` the same way before the first club email; a return path of our own lets SPF line up with the From domain for DMARC under relaxed alignment. Each of the two subdomains also needs an A or MX record, so that a From domain exists in DNS (Yahoo's sender rules), and the key's policy gains the two new From addresses and the new identities (the checklist's section 11).

## Risks

- **Amazon's answer.** The request for real sending can take longer than 96 hours or be refused; until it comes, the switch-on can still go on with Resend carrying event and account mail, and the first newsletter waits. The honest text in the checklist (both kinds of mail, who the newsletter goes to, how bounces and complaints are handled) gives it the best chance.
- **Amazon's review of a new account.** The first newsletter is most of a new account's volume, so one or two spam reports can pass 0.1%. The list goes to current members only, Resend's suppressed addresses are put on Amazon's list first, the example domains are skipped, and alarms tell K early.
- **Sending twice.** The engine's rules make it impossible by design; their cost is that an unclear answer leaves one person without that message, which the history shows.
- **Widening who sends.** The media manager's grant reads the seat at each request, needs a second factor, ends with the term and reaches only WS16's own endpoints; the integration test walks every other part of Admin and all five of today's campaign methods to show they are refused there.
- **A broken link in sent mail.** The links never expire, a previous key keeps them through a change of key, each deployment checks its copy of the key, the API keeps answering once a list email has gone, and the pages are always routed.
- **The receiver going quiet.** SNS discards a notification after its tries; the delivery policy stretches them to about 50 minutes, a certificate the code cannot read answers 500, and an alarm on failed deliveries emails K. A subscription removed through a notification's unsubscribe address is not caught by that alarm; the receiver logs the confirmation SNS sends, and the topic's page shows the subscription (the checklist).
- **The free plan.** The processor limit is not measured on Cloudflare; SES sends only on Workers Paid, and the daily run's budget keeps reminders inside 50 outside calls. If Workers Paid lapses while SES sends, `MAIL_SENDER` goes back to `'resend'` until it is restored.
- **Amazon's own bounce emails.** Amazon emails bounce and complaint notices to the From address (or the return path) unless email feedback forwarding is turned off. Today no address at the domain receives mail, so they have nowhere to go; once choice 6 gives the root a working MX they would be refused, if Cloudflare's forwarding has no rule for the From address, or fill the officers' inbox. The plan takes the reports through the topic (step 8), and the checklist turns forwarding off at part B step 9, before the root can receive mail.
- **Names that must exist.** Yahoo's sender rules ask that a From domain exist in DNS with an A or MX record, and `news.` and `clubs.` have none yet; each gets an A record in the checklist's section 11. The return-path names are `bounce.news.` and `bounce.clubs.`, never `news.` or `clubs.` themselves.
- **The old web host's leftovers.** An old DKIM key at `default._domainkey` and a root SPF record naming two old addresses could still be used to send as LCA; the checklist's section 12 removes both, safely for the site's mail, before DMARC is ever tightened from `p=none`.
- **DKIM over the one-click headers.** RFC 8058 wants the DKIM signature to cover both headers; K's test shows it in the h= list of the signature whose d= is louisianachess.org. If not, Gmail may not show its own button, and the footer's link still meets the FTC's rule.
