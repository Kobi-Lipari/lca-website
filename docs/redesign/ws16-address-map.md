## 2. The naming rule

1. **One domain, three sending names.** Account and event mail comes from louisianachess.org itself, LCA's lists from news.louisianachess.org, clubs' lists from clubs.louisianachess.org. (A subdomain is a name in front of the domain, like news.) A new list gets a new first part on one of these, never a new subdomain. Renewal reminders are account mail, from noreply@, as the plan reads decision 18.
2. **Each name keeps its own standing.** Mail providers score the domain that signs each message. So news. and clubs. sign with keys of their own (choice 3), and each list keeps its own unsubscribe.
3. **The sender says what the mail is, and replies reach a person.** The display name (the name an inbox shows) names the association, the list or the club. Every reply reaches a mailbox someone reads. Today no address at louisianachess.org or under it receives mail, so none is ever a recipient, a reply-to, a club's mailbox or a contact given to Amazon, Stripe or Supabase. This holds until K decides choice 6: if K picks route (a) or (b) there, the reply address and the contact address may move to a domain address in one line of `domain/mail/addresses.ts`, and no list is sent to an address at the domain either way.
4. **Nothing a member has seen changes,** except two changes already planned: receipts move from Stripe to LCA (WS06), and a club's page address gives way to the contact form (D29). An address, once used, stays. Links in emails stay on www.louisianachess.org.

## 3. What members never see

These DNS records (the domain's public settings, kept at Cloudflare) show only in a message's technical details.

- **Return path** (where bounce reports go): bounce.louisianachess.org, and, as choice 3 is decided, bounce.news.louisianachess.org now and bounce.clubs.louisianachess.org with the clubs set-up. Two records each. All are new. Each is a name of its own under its sending domain, never news. or clubs. itself: Amazon says the return-path name must not be a name you send From and must be used for nothing else. Never a sender or a mailbox.
- **DKIM** (a public key that lets receivers check our signature): three records with `._domainkey` in their names for the domain, three more for news. (choice 3, decided), and three for clubs. later. Amazon makes them.
- **DMARC** (it tells receivers what to do with mail that fails those checks): "monitor only" (p=none), reporting to Cloudflare. It covers the subdomains and stays.
- **Root SPF** (the servers allowed to send as the domain): two old IP addresses, most likely the old web host's, and nothing for Amazon or Cloudflare. The site's mail does not use it: Amazon's mail passes DMARC through its DKIM signature, and its return path is a name of its own. It is an old-host leftover: K's checklist, section 12, removes it, safely for the site's mail, and before DMARC is ever tightened.
- **Resend's records** (send.louisianachess.org and resend._domainkey): deleted a week after step 12 merges, no earlier than about Friday, December 25, 2026, and only after the sign-in emails leave Resend (choice 7).
- **Old host leftovers** (read in public DNS on Wednesday, November 11, 2026): an old DKIM key at default._domainkey, the root SPF above, an autodiscover SRV record pointing at the old host's mail set-up service, the names mail., webmail., cpanel., ftp., autodiscover. and autoconfig. (which DNS shows only as Cloudflare's addresses, like the website), and the broken root MX, which leads to the website and waits for choice 6. The site's mail needs none of them. The old key and the two old addresses could still be used to send as LCA, so K's checklist, section 12, removes the key and the SPF record.
- **Names that must exist.** Yahoo asks that a From domain exist in DNS with an A or MX record. news. and clubs. do not exist yet, so each gets one before it is sent From (checklist, section 11). Do not publish a null MX: Yahoo lists it as a reason to refuse mail.

**6. Where should replies go, and should LCA move to a more official address?** Open, at K's question on Wednesday, November 11, 2026: "would it be best to start going to another more official email, and how would we do that? in amazon ses? or what?" The answer, from the lead's research (four angles and two checkers, read on Wednesday, November 11, 2026):

- Amazon is not the place. Amazon SES only sends: it has no inbox that a person opens. Amazon's one mailbox product, WorkMail, took no new customers after Thursday, April 30, 2026 and ends on Wednesday, March 31, 2027.
- The recommended route is (a), if LCA holds the status it needs. The options, recommended first:
  - **(a) Google Workspace for Nonprofits.** Recommended if LCA holds its own IRS 501(c)(3) determination or is covered by a 501(c)(3) group exemption. Why: it is the only free route that gives a real mailbox at the domain, such as info@louisianachess.org, in Gmail, the screen the officers already know; a reply shows the official address; one shared address can be read by three or four officers through delegation or a group inbox. Cost: $0 per user per month. Time: one to three weeks, mostly waiting. Google's partner Goodstack checks eligibility (3 to 5 business days by one Google page, up to 14 by another), then Workspace activation takes a further several days. Set-up: prove the domain with one TXT record, replace the broken root MX with one record (smtp.google.com, priority 1; up to 72 hours to spread), add Google's include to the root SPF, switch on Google's DKIM, make two people super admins so a change of officers never locks LCA out. Eligibility: in the United States, only IRS 501(c)(3) organisations or members of a 501(c)(3) group exemption; a state non-profit registration alone is not enough, nor is being fiscally sponsored without a status of one's own. New accounts are capped at 300 users.
  - **(b) Cloudflare Email Routing.** Free; 15 to 30 minutes; an address at the domain (up to 200) that forwards into the Gmail inbox LCA has. Receive only: a reply an officer writes still shows the Gmail address. Set-up: delete the broken root MX and the old root SPF record, switch Email Routing on (it adds three MX records, one SPF record and one DKIM record at the root and locks them), and make one rule per address in the same sitting, because mail to an address with no rule is refused. Leave the catch-all off. Test from another account, not from the Gmail that receives. Cloudflare also refuses mail that fails both SPF and DKIM, fails the sender's own DMARC policy, or comes from a block-listed address, so a club or parent on a poorly set up mail system could bounce. Gain: the addresses belong to the domain and can be pointed at another inbox in minutes, by whoever holds the Cloudflare login. It does not touch mail sent through Amazon.
  - **(c) Stay as now.** Replies go to LouisianaChess@gmail.com and no address at the domain receives mail. Cost: nothing. The broken root MX stays. Yahoo's rule (a From domain must exist with an A or MX record) is met by the root's A records, and Microsoft only recommends that a From or Reply-To address can receive replies, so no one could show that it lowers delivery today.
- (a) and (b) are either-or at the root, since both need the root MX; Cloudflare says Email Routing "requires exclusive control of the root domain MX records". If (a) is likely within weeks, skip (b) and go straight there. If (b) goes first and (a) later: every forwarded address must first exist in Workspace as a user, alias or group; then Email Routing's records are unlocked, Google's records are added, and only then is Email Routing switched off; Cloudflare's own MX records are never edited in place (Cloudflare says editing them does not detach them: they are still deleted when routing is switched off). Do not fix the broken MX with a null MX: Yahoo lists it as a reason to refuse mail.
- Ruled out, and why:
  - Amazon SES and WorkMail, as above.
  - Gmail's "Send mail as" through Amazon: Google's help pages say it ends in January 2027 for addresses Google does not host, and that new set-ups are restricted before then. It would also need the address to receive mail first, since Gmail sends a confirmation link to it, and it puts a long-lived Amazon password inside a shared Gmail account.
  - Microsoft's free plan (365 Business Basic): it needs the same kind of tax status, means learning Outlook, grants free seats only to paid staff and unpaid leadership, and its rules exclude professional and semiprofessional sports bodies and trade associations, which Microsoft decides at its own discretion.
  - Zoho's free plan (5 users, web only, no forwarding): not confirmed for a new US sign-up, since Zoho says only that it is offered in selected data centers. If it is not offered, the account is a 15-day trial and the cheapest plan is $1 per user per month, billed yearly.
  - If LCA has no 501(c)(3) status and wants a real mailbox: one paid Google Workspace seat at $7 per user per month on an annual plan ($8.40 on a flexible plan); in a Workspace account delegates must be accounts in the same organisation, so sharing needs a seat per officer or a group. Route (b) costs nothing.
- **The one fact needed from K:** does LCA hold its own IRS 501(c)(3) determination letter, or is it covered by a group exemption? The treasurer's letter settles it in one look. Nobody has looked LCA up by name. If yes, pick (a). If not, pick (b), or (c) for now.
- **The site does not wait on this.** The reply address is one line in `domain/mail/addresses.ts`, and replies keep going to LouisianaChess@gmail.com until K decides. If K picks (a) or (b), the line and the contact address change together, in a small change of their own.
- **A free tip, whatever K picks:** a personal Gmail account can have up to 10 delegates (Google's page on delegating access), so the officers can each open LCA's inbox from their own Gmail sign-in, today, without sharing its password. A delegate's own address shows on what they send.
- Once the root can receive mail, Amazon emails bounce and complaint notices to a message's From address unless email feedback forwarding is turned off. The plan takes those notices through its own topic (step 8), and K's checklist says when to turn forwarding off.
