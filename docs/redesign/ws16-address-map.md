## 2. The naming rule

1. **One domain, three sending names.** Account and event mail comes from louisianachess.org itself, LCA's lists from news.louisianachess.org, clubs' lists from clubs.louisianachess.org. (A subdomain is a name in front of the domain, like news.) A new list gets a new first part on one of these, never a new subdomain. Renewal reminders are account mail, from noreply@, as the plan reads decision 18.
2. **Each name keeps its own standing.** Mail providers score the domain that signs each message. So news. and clubs. sign with keys of their own (choice 3), and each list keeps its own unsubscribe.
3. **The sender says what the mail is, and replies reach a person.** The display name (the name an inbox shows) names the association, the list or the club. Every reply reaches a mailbox someone reads. No address at louisianachess.org or under it receives mail, so none is ever a recipient, a reply-to, a club's mailbox or a contact given to Amazon, Stripe or Supabase.
4. **Nothing a member has seen changes,** except two changes already planned: receipts move from Stripe to LCA (WS06), and a club's page address gives way to the contact form (D29). An address, once used, stays. Links in emails stay on www.louisianachess.org.

## 3. What members never see

These DNS records (the domain's public settings, kept at Cloudflare) show only in a message's technical details.

- **Return path** (where bounce reports go): bounce.louisianachess.org; with choice 3, also bounce.news.louisianachess.org now and bounce.clubs.louisianachess.org with the clubs set-up. Two records each. Never a sender or a mailbox. All are new.
- **DKIM** (a public key that lets receivers check our signature): three records with `._domainkey` in their names for the domain, three more for news. (choice 3), and three for clubs. later. Amazon makes them.
- **DMARC** (it tells receivers what to do with mail that fails those checks): "monitor only" (p=none), reporting to Cloudflare. It covers the subdomains and stays.
- **Root SPF** (the servers allowed to send as the domain): two outside servers, most likely the old web host's, plus the addresses the website and the domain's mail record lead to. It stays.
- **Resend's records** (send.louisianachess.org and resend._domainkey): deleted a week after step 12 merges, no earlier than about Friday, December 25, 2026, and only after the sign-in emails leave Resend (choice 7).
- **Old host leftovers** (default._domainkey; mail., webmail., autodiscover., ftp. and cpanel., which DNS shows only as Cloudflare's addresses, like the website): unused by the site, though the old key and the SPF's two servers could still send as LCA. This work leaves them alone.

## 4. Your choices

**1. What should the newsletter show as its sender?** Recommended: (a) `Louisiana Chess Association <newsletter@news.louisianachess.org>`. Why: it is the name every LCA email already carries, and the name on the option boards you send the president on Wednesday, November 11, 2026. The subject names the newsletter, as decision 23 asks, so an inbox reads Louisiana Chess Association, then LCA Newsletter · December 2026. Gmail asks for a consistent name that says who sends, with no subject words in it. Other options: (b) "LCA Newsletter", which repeats the subject's first words and would mean changing the boards' sender line before they go; (c) "Louisiana Chess Association Newsletter", cut short on a phone. Cost: none; it is the plan's own form.

**2. What should LCA tournament email show as its sender, and what is its list called?** Recommended: (a) `LCA Tournaments <tournaments@news.louisianachess.org>`, with the list named "LCA tournament email" (the plan's question 7, which stands unless you say otherwise), if groups of members get only tournament news. Why: it is LCA's own list, so it belongs on news.louisianachess.org; its subjects vary, so the name says what it is; and it is your own name for it (decision 18). Step 11 also lets this list carry other LCA news to a group, such as a note to every club representative; if you will use it that way, pick (b) now, so the sender never changes. Other options: (b) `LCA Announcements <announcements@news.louisianachess.org>`, with a wider list name, such as the plan's "Email from LCA"; (c) the newsletter's address, which mislabels it; (d) noreply@, which mixes it with account and event mail. Cost: one entry in the plan's address file, which step 11 already adds.

**3. Should LCA's lists be signed apart from account and event mail?** Recommended: (a) yes: news.louisianachess.org as its own sending domain at Amazon now, clubs.louisianachess.org before the first club email. Why: Yahoo's guide says "Each IP and DKIM domain has a reputation" and asks senders to separate email types by it; with one key, mail providers would count spam reports on one issue against both. Amazon still counts every spam report against the whole account (a review at 0.1%, a possible pause at 0.5%), and a pause stops account mail too. Other option: (b) the plan's single key: five fewer records, one shared score. Cost: no code. One more domain set-up at Amazon with the main domain's settings (your checklist's section 11), five DNS records and one line in the sending key's permissions: about 15 minutes, best done now, since records can take 72 hours. clubs. costs the same later.

**4. What form does a club's address take, and may a club move?** Recommended: (a) the club's name at its id, the end of its page address: `Baton Rouge Chess Club <baton-rouge@clubs.louisianachess.org>`. Replies go to the club's own mailbox, a working address outside louisianachess.org, which must be on record before the club sends. A club never moves to a subdomain of its own. Why: the id survives a rename, and a move would change an address members know. A club drawing complaints is paused instead. Other options: (b) one shared address for all clubs; (c) decision 6's "split out later", with one address change for that club. Cost: a sentence in decision 6 and in the plan's part two outline.

**5. Where should a representative's mail come from?** Recommended: (a) no address of its own. It goes out on each club's list from that club's address, naming the representative inside. Why: people see the club they joined and its unsubscribe. Other option: (b) a region address on clubs., with a region list, which decision 4 does not have. Cost: none now; part two confirms it.

**6. Should replies keep going to LouisianaChess@gmail.com?** Recommended: (a) yes, for all of LCA's own mail. Why: it is LCA's only working mailbox and the address on the site. Other option: (b) a new contact@louisianachess.org forwarding there: more official, but a second address to learn, and it changes the domain's mail record, which this work leaves alone. Cost: none.

**7. What happens to the sign-in emails when Resend goes?** Recommended: (a) check, then move only the host. In Supabase open Authentication, then Email, then SMTP Settings (the path in Resend's guide), and note the sender, its name and the host. If the host is smtp.resend.com, these emails stop when Resend goes, so the host moves first to Amazon's mail-server connection (SMTP), with a key of its own whose permission names that sender. The sender and its name stay if the address is on louisianachess.org; only a sender outside it becomes `Louisiana Chess Association <noreply@louisianachess.org>`, once. Why: only the host has to change, and everyone who signed up has seen the sender; the plan does not cover these emails yet. Other options: (b) leave it, safe only if it does not use Resend; (c) move them to the account sender anyway, a change for no need. Cost: one checklist item.
